Sec. 320. SALES AND USE TAX ON OUT-OF-STATE VEHICLE TRANSFERS.

(a) The transfer of a motor vehicle previously titled outside the State of Marion to a
Marion title, whether by purchase or otherwise, is subject to Marion sales and use tax.
The applicable tax rate and the method of determining taxable value shall be as prescribed
by the Administrator of the Department of Revenue in coordination with the Marion Department
of Motor Vehicles (see Admin. Rule 7).

(b) For purposes of this section, "purchase" means any acquisition of a vehicle by a natural
person or legal entity in exchange for consideration, including trade-in transactions.

(c) Taxable value shall be determined by the greater of:
    (1) the declared purchase price as reflected on a bill of sale, purchase agreement,
        or equivalent instrument; or
    (2) the fair market value as published in a motor vehicle valuation guide approved
        by the Administrator.

Where no purchase transaction exists (see § 321, relocation), taxable value is determined
solely by reference to the approved valuation guide.

(d) Tax is due and payable at the time of application for the Marion title. Unpaid tax
shall be subject to the late penalty established in Admin. Rule 9.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Sec. 321. RELOCATION; USE TAX ON VEHICLES BROUGHT INTO MARION.

(a) An owner of a motor vehicle previously titled in another jurisdiction who establishes
Marion residency and brings the vehicle to Marion for registration shall owe Marion use tax
on the vehicle.

(b) For relocation transfers, the taxable value is determined by reference to the
Administrator-approved motor vehicle valuation guide (NADA Clean Retail value as of the
date of transfer application), not by purchase price, because no new purchase transaction
has occurred.

(c) The owner must provide proof that Marion residency has been established within ninety
(90) days prior to the title application. Proof requirements are as specified in Admin.
Rule 2.1 and the Examiner Procedure Manual.

(d) All other provisions of § 320, including the obligation to pay tax at time of
application and the applicable penalty for late payment, apply to relocation transfers.

Sec. 322. RECIPROCITY AGREEMENTS; CREDIT FOR TAX PAID IN ORIGIN STATE.

(a) The Director of Revenue is hereby authorized to enter into reciprocity agreements
with other states or jurisdictions providing for the avoidance of double taxation on
motor vehicle transfers.

(b) Where a valid reciprocity agreement exists between Marion and the origin jurisdiction:
    (1) The applicant shall receive a credit against the Marion tax owed, in an amount
        equal to the lesser of:
        (a) the sales or use tax verifiably paid by the applicant to the origin
            jurisdiction on the same vehicle; or
        (b) the Marion tax owed on the taxable value of the vehicle.
    (2) If the credit computed under paragraph (1) equals or exceeds the Marion tax owed,
        the additional tax due to Marion shall be zero (0). Marion does not issue refunds
        for credits exceeding the Marion tax owed.
    (3) Acceptable proof of tax paid to the origin state includes the original bill of
        sale with tax line itemized, a tax receipt issued by the origin jurisdiction, or
        other documentation as specified by the Administrator.

(c) Where no reciprocity agreement exists between Marion and the origin jurisdiction,
no credit shall be given for any amount of sales or use tax paid to that jurisdiction.
The full Marion tax applies regardless of taxes previously paid. The Administrator
shall maintain and publish the Marion Tax Reciprocity Schedule identifying jurisdictions
with which Marion has entered agreements (see Admin. Rule 7 and the Tax Reciprocity
Schedule published by the Department of Revenue).

(d) The existence or absence of a reciprocity agreement shall be determined solely by
reference to the current Tax Reciprocity Schedule. Examiners shall not make independent
determinations of reciprocity.
