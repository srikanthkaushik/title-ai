Sec. 310. TITLE BRAND DEFINITIONS.

For purposes of this Code and all rules promulgated hereunder, the following brand
designations are hereby established for use on Marion motor vehicle titles:

    (1) "Salvage" — a motor vehicle that has been declared a total loss by an insurer,
        financial institution, or self-insured entity, or that has been acquired for
        salvage purposes. A salvage vehicle may not be titled for road use in Marion
        unless it has been rebuilt and inspected as provided by rule.

    (2) "Reconstructed" — a motor vehicle that was previously branded Salvage and has
        been rebuilt to an operable, roadworthy condition in compliance with applicable
        safety standards, and has passed a Marion salvage inspection. A Reconstructed
        vehicle is road-legal in Marion.

    (3) "Rebuilt" — a motor vehicle that has been rebuilt or restored, but which was
        NOT previously branded Salvage. Rebuilt vehicles have been repaired using
        replacement components (including non-OEM parts) but did not originate from
        a salvage declaration. Distinguished from Reconstructed in that no prior total-
        loss or salvage condition existed.

    (4) "Flood Damage" — a motor vehicle that has sustained water immersion damage
        to the vehicle's passenger compartment, trunk, or engine compartment. Applies
        regardless of whether a total-loss declaration was made.

    (5) "Odometer Rollback" — a motor vehicle whose odometer has been altered, reset,
        or tampered with so that it does not reflect the actual mileage.

    (6) "Lemon Law Buyback" — a motor vehicle that has been returned to a manufacturer,
        distributor, or dealer pursuant to a state or federal lemon law statute, consumer
        protection agreement, or manufacturer warranty settlement.

Sec. 311. DUTY TO CARRY BRANDS; PERMANENCE.

(a) Any brand appearing on a motor vehicle's title is a permanent encumbrance on that
vehicle's title history. A Marion title issued for a vehicle bearing a brand shall carry
that brand, and all subsequent Marion titles for that vehicle shall carry that brand.

(b) No Marion title examiner, supervisor, or officer of the Department may issue a title
that omits a brand that appears on the vehicle's title history, whether such brand
originated in Marion or in another jurisdiction.

(c) A title brand may not be removed or modified except by order of a Marion court of
competent jurisdiction or upon a showing, in accordance with rules promulgated by
the Administrator, that the brand was applied in error.

(d) Where more than one brand applies, all applicable brands shall be carried on the
Marion title.

Sec. 312. BRAND EQUIVALENCY; ADMINISTRATOR'S SCHEDULE.

(a) The Administrator of the Marion Department of Motor Vehicles shall maintain and publish
a Brand Equivalency Schedule (hereinafter "the Schedule") mapping brand designations used
by other jurisdictions to the Marion brand nomenclature established in § 310.

(b) The Schedule shall be binding on all examiners and supervisors. Where the origin
jurisdiction used a brand term not identical to a Marion brand term, the Marion equivalent
as shown in the Schedule shall be applied to the Marion title.

(c) A brand designation used by an origin jurisdiction that does not appear in the Schedule
shall be treated as an unrecognized brand. Vehicles bearing an unrecognized brand are
subject to mandatory supervisor review under Admin. Rule 5.2(b) before a Marion title
is issued. The Administrator shall update the Schedule annually or as needed to address
emerging brand designations.

(d) Vehicles subject to supervisor review under this section, or under §§ 305 or 311, shall
not receive a finalized document checklist or final fee computation from a title examiner
until the supervisor has cleared the transaction. The Administrator may by rule (see Admin.
Rule 2.5 — Brand Equivalency Schedule) specify the form of the Schedule and procedures for
its application.

(e) The existence of a supervisor review requirement under this section does not prevent
an examiner from providing the applicant with a conditional list of likely required
documents, clearly marked as conditional and subject to supervisor review, consistent
with the procedures established in Admin. Rule 5.3.
